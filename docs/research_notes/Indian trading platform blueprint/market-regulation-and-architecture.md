# Indian market requirements and low latency architecture evidence

Research reference date: **21 October 2026, Asia/Kolkata**. Sources accessed 21 October 2026. This is a planning evidence file, not confirmation that an account or deployment is approved. R identifiers are external facts; T identifiers are technology facts. The architecture recommendations under Inferences are proposed design decisions, not measured results.

## Which Indian regulatory and market rules constrain this platform?

### Takeaway

The retail algo framework is already applicable, but the precise compliance route depends on whether this is an investor's own system or an algo-provider offering. Date-versioned market calendars are essential: current CAS and F&O hours differ from older assumptions, and a newly issued October 2026 circular schedules further changes for December 2026 and February 2027.

### Cited Findings

#### Regulatory evidence ledger

- **R01 — Retail algo framework and effective timeline.** SEBI's 30 September 2025 circular states that the February 2025 framework and exchange implementation standards apply to all stockbrokers from **1 April 2026**. It describes October/November 2025 registration milestones and a January 2026 broker mock milestone; these are historical transition obligations, not future implementation dates. The circular identifies the original 4 February 2025 framework and a previous July extension. [SEBI circular SEBI/HO/MIRSD/MIRSD-PoD/P/CIR/2025/132, pp. 1–2](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/sep-2025/1759232056254.pdf)
- **R02 — Foundational client API standards.** NSE's 5 May 2025 standards initially set the threshold at **not exceeding 10 orders per second**, using the broker's calendar-clock second; section B describes per-exchange scope and section F says exchange/segment. Brokers can impose lower client limits. Above-threshold client algos require registration through the broker for the relevant exchange. The standards cover primary/secondary static IPs, daily session logout, API identity, 2FA and broker risk controls. They state a minimum five-year broker order/trade audit trail. These are exchange requirements on the relevant participants, not proof that every private application has identical legal retention duties. Later clarifications must be applied. [NSE/INVG/67858, sections A–J](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/INVG67858.pdf)
- **R03 — Later clarification on account route and tagging.** NSE's November 2025 FAQ says all client API orders are algo orders and require tagging, including those within the threshold. It distinguishes tech-savvy clients, whose logic can run at their own static IP, from provider strategies on trading-member servers. Black-box providers must also register as research analysts and maintain research reports. The FAQ also describes market-order restrictions and warns that binding circulars prevail over FAQ text. [NSE Retail Algo FAQ, November 2025, questions 3–11](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/FAQ_Retail%20Algo_03112025_NSE.pdf)
- **R04 — 2026 corroboration.** NSE's 22 April 2026 system-audit terms repeat daily API logout and whitelisted IP controls. Clause 27 exempts tech-savvy clients from the broker-hosting requirement. It explicitly says below-threshold client-developed algorithms do not require individual exchange registration but still receive generic algo tagging; above-threshold client algorithms require registration. Provider algorithms require empanelment and registration. [NSE/INSP/73850, clause 27, pp. 55–57](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/INSP73850.pdf)
- **R05 — Original SEBI source identity.** The original framework is circular SEBI/HO/MIRSD/MIRSD-PoD/P/CIR/2025/0000013, dated 4 February 2025. [SEBI original circular landing page](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/feb-2025/safer-participation-of-retail-investors-in-algorithmic-trading_91614.html)

#### Session and instrument evidence ledger

- **R06 — Current segment hours.** NSE's current market-timings page lists normal equity trading at 09:15–15:30 and equity derivatives at 09:15–15:40. The equity summary does not resolve all CAS-eligible security phases. Do not apply its generic equity close to every eligible instrument's continuous session. [NSE market timings](https://www.nseindia.com/static/market-data/market-timings)
- **R07 — Current CAS implementation date.** NSE's 30 July 2026 circular explicitly says the equity-derivatives changes associated with CAS become live on **3 August 2026**, referring to NSE/FAOP/74467 of 29 May and CMTR/73362 of 18 March. Its listed mock-session times are test-session times, not the production calendar. [NSE/FAOP/75472, p. 1](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/FAOP75472.pdf)
- **R08 — Current broker-visible CAS capabilities.** Upstox's announcement, effective 3 August 2026, adds `cas_eligible` to equity instruments and optional `cas_eligible_status` to exchange status. Its approximate sequence is CTS close 15:15, auction limit/market entry 15:20, market restriction 15:25, random entry close 15:28–15:30. SL/SL-M, IOC and slicing are disallowed during CAS; GTT triggers for these securities run only until 15:15. It reports NSE_FO/BSE_FO normal close at 15:40. These are the documented Upstox states, not universal timestamps for every exchange message. [Upstox CAS support](https://upstox.com/developer/api-documentation/announcements/closing-auction-session/)
- **R09 — Current reference price and band.** NSE's March 2026 CAS SOP initially applies to cash stocks with derivative contracts. It uses 15:00–15:15 VWAP as reference, with last-trade/previous-close fallbacks, and prescribes a ±3% CAS band. Stock-futures bands align during 15:15–15:40. The source also specifies corporate-action handling for the previous close fallback. [NSE/CMTR/73362, pp. 1–2](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/CMTR73362.pdf)
- **R10 — Prospective changes, NOT current as of 21 October.** SEBI's 15 October 2026 circular takes effect **7 December 2026**, except paragraph 4.8 carryover changes on **8 February 2027**. It schedules CAS 15:30–15:40, market+limit entry from 15:31, limit-only from 15:36, random close 15:38–15:40. Stock derivatives continue until 15:45; index derivatives until 15:45 except expiring contracts close 15:30. It changes expiry settlement to underlying/constituent VWAP over the last 30 minutes of continuous trading and sets post-close cash trading 15:50–16:00. Paragraph 4.8 concerns pending limit/iceberg carryover. [SEBI HO/47/11/11(3)2025-MRD-POD2/I/23843/2026, pp. 2–6](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/oct-2026/1792079571420.pdf)
- **R11 — Revised derivatives pre-open.** NSE's page updated 4 September 2026 describes 09:00–09:05 market and limit entry, 09:05–09:10 limit-only with random close in the last two minutes, matching to 09:12 and buffer to 09:15. Current-month stock/index futures qualify; next-month futures are added in the last five trading days before expiry. It explicitly allows algo market orders during the initial five-minute window. This is a session-specific exception to treating the older retail FAQ as a timeless blanket market-order ban. [NSE equity derivatives pre-open](https://www.nseindia.com/static/products-services/equity-derivatives-pre-open-session)
- **R12 — Derivative metadata is dynamic.** NSE publishes quantity-freeze and permitted-lot files. The stock-F&O page describes last-Tuesday expiry, moved to the preceding trading day on a holiday, and points to `NSE_FO_contract_ddmmyyyy.csv.gz` for current lot/freeze details. Do not calculate all contracts from a weekday rule. [NSE contract information](https://www.nseindia.com/static/products-services/equity-derivatives-contract-information); [NSE individual securities F&O](https://www.nseindia.com/static/products-services/equity-derivatives-individual-securities)
- **R13 — Stock derivatives create delivery obligations.** NSE's 2021 circular confirms mandatory physical settlement of stock derivatives from October 2019 expiries and requires brokers to make settlement available to clients who wish to use it, with disclosed internal risk policies. Broker square-off policy and the platform's own intraday exits are distinct controls. [NSE/INSP/47293](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/INSP47293.pdf)
- **R14 — Short-selling restrictions.** SEBI's trading chapter permits short selling by investor classes but prohibits naked short selling and requires delivery obligations to be honored at settlement. Institutional investors cannot day trade under this framework. The platform must not treat all accounts or every instrument as eligible for retail intraday shorts. [SEBI trading chapter, short-selling framework](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/commondocs/dec-2024/RE_Chapter%201%20-%20Trading%20-%20NEW_p.pdf)
- **R15 — Position-ban controls.** NSE's position-limits page states that crossing 95% of market-wide limits triggers restrictions to position reduction from the next day; normal trading resumes at 80% or below. Use the exchange/broker published ban state rather than rebuilding a legally authoritative ban solely from incomplete retail OI feeds. [NSE equity derivatives position limits](https://www.nseindia.com/static/products-services/equity-derivatives-position-limits)
- **R16 — Current STT change.** NSE lists STT effective 1 April 2026: option sale **0.15%** of premium, exercised option **0.15%** of intrinsic value paid by purchaser, and futures sale **0.05%** paid by seller. Older 0.10% option sale and 0.02% futures sale rates are obsolete for this reference date. Brokerage, GST, stamp duty, exchange charges and other levies remain separately versioned inputs; this is not a complete fee schedule. [NSE STT schedule](https://www.nseindia.com/static/products-services/equity-derivatives-securities-transaction-tax)

#### Sector classification and licensing evidence ledger

- **R17 — Official sectors are a maintained catalog.** NSE Indices publishes sectoral methodology links, including Auto, Bank, Financial Services, FMCG, IT, Media, Metal, Pharma, Private Bank, PSU Bank, Realty, Consumer Durables, Oil & Gas, Healthcare, Chemicals and further sector indices. Its industry-classification page describes four levels. Sector indices, official industry taxonomy and user-defined groupings are different datasets. [NSE Indices methodology catalog](https://www.niftyindices.com/resources/index-methodology); [NSE industry classification](https://www.niftyindices.com/resources/industry-classification)
- **R18 — Licensing is use-specific.** NSE Indices describes licensing for products based on/linked to its indices, benchmarking uses and trademarks. This does not itself prove that broker-feed access authorizes public redistribution, commercial dashboards or derived-data redistribution. [NSE Indices licensing](https://www.niftyindices.com/offerings/index-licensing)

### Inferences

The following are recommended platform specifications, not additional regulatory assertions:

1. **Deployment assumption:** design initially as one investor's self-developed platform using that investor's enabled DhanHQ and Upstox accounts. Preserve separate account identities and entitlements. This is an assumption to validate before production, not a declaration that the user qualifies as a tech-savvy client. A public or third-party commercial offering requires a new compliance review and potentially broker-hosted deployment/empanelment; do not silently broaden this scope.
2. **Go-live policy object:** `ComplianceProfile` contains legal-account category, broker/account, enabled exchanges/segments, API identity, approved primary/secondary egress IP, threshold policy, algo registration/tag mapping, order-type/session rules, permitted products, effective dates, evidence references, reviewer and expiry of verification. Missing required evidence means observation-only operation for that route; no implied authorization from successful login.
3. **Rate scheduling:** maintain an exchange/segment regulatory message budget AND broker endpoint/account budgets. Use the stricter verified scope until broker clarification. Count new/modify/cancel messages conservatively, including child slices, and record how the broker counts them. Do not mistake 10 OPS for ten trades per day, scanner capacity or a system-wide fixed opportunity limit. Apply a conservative smooth limiter and broker-clock-boundary guard; administrative increase only after evidence of approval.
4. **Credentials:** secrets stay server-side. Interactive broker login/2FA remains broker-managed. Do not promise unattended token renewal when the broker demands reauthorization. At required daily logout, stop new entries, invalidate sessions, reconnect/read-only reconcile and resume only after renewed entitlement and account-state checks.
5. **Session model:** `ExchangeSessionRule` is keyed by exchange, segment, instrument/session category, effective-from/to, session date, timezone, phase and source version. `InstrumentSessionState` merges that calendar with authoritative status events and instrument eligibility. Fields include `orders_allowed`, `allowed_types`, `cancel_allowed`, `modify_allowed`, auction phase, random-close interval, market-data expectations and broker square-off deadline. Unknown/conflicting schedule blocks new entries and raises an actionable alert.
6. **Calendar versions:** preserve CURRENT_2026_08_03_CAS separately from PENDING_2026_12_07_CAS and PENDING_2027_02_08_CARRYOVER. Activation requires current exchange operational notices and broker support. Future dates are not enabled early. Read actual session states rather than predicting the moment of random close.
7. **Candle boundaries:** construct continuous-session bars per instrument category. Do not silently merge auction prices into ordinary intraday bars, assume a universal 375-minute session, or infer final daily close from the final streaming LTP. Persist phase, source and reconciliation status; auction/final-close observations can form distinct records used to reconcile official daily candles.
8. **Order types:** LIMIT and supported SL-LIMIT are the initial automated policy. Represent MARKET, SL-M, IOC and broker-specific protections in the contract but gate by account, segment, phase and verified current policy. A broker API's enumeration alone is not authorization to submit that order type. Stops may be unavailable during CAS; intraday strategies must plan exits/protection before phase transitions.
9. **No hardcoded derivative metadata:** load expiry, underlying, lot size, tick size, freeze quantity, series and settlement type from dated exchange/broker masters. Conflicting masters quarantine the mapping. Lot/strike changes and expiry events invalidate derived option candidates.
10. **Restricted universe:** retain all instruments in discovery, separately label live-data coverage, scanner readiness and order eligibility. Published F&O ban/ASM/GSM/trade-to-trade/price-band/broker restrictions exclude new entries while allowing only explicitly validated reducing actions. Unknown eligibility is not inferred from having a price.
11. **Cost model:** use effective-dated schedules keyed by broker, exchange, segment, product and side. Compute estimated entry and exit fees plus spread and a separately labeled slippage estimate. Unit tests use versioned fixtures including 31 March versus 1 April 2026 STT boundaries. Do not embed a net-profit claim.
12. **Sector imports:** maintain official index ID, constituent set, weighting methodology/version, validity dates, corporate-action state and license provenance. A calculated equal-weight or custom-sector heatmap must be labeled as a platform calculation; it is not the official index return. Never scrape a web list as an unqualified real-time production entitlement.
13. **Retention policy:** propose five years minimum for application order decisions/intents/rejections/fills/audit evidence to align operational auditability, subject to verified applicable account/business obligations. Keep credential values out of logs and immutable exports. Tick storage duration is a separate cost/entitlement decision, not automatically five years.
14. **Instrument/account acceptance:** verify small-unit contract conformance and authorized live order checks only after user-controlled launch authorization, credentials, margin, segment activation and broker regulatory requirements are satisfied. Planning and offline protocol fixtures do not create a paper-trading subsystem.

### Gaps

- BSE's original notice endpoint and attachment returned 403. Do not claim independent verification of every BSE implementation detail from NSE text. SEBI framework and Upstox's explicitly documented BSE behavior are cited, but current BSE-specific operational circulars, order restrictions and approval route remain a production gate.
- NSE/FAOP/74467 could not be fetched directly; its identity and live date are corroborated by accessible NSE/FAOP/75472 and the current NSE timings page. Upstox independently specifies the changed close and effective date. Archive the operative circular before enabling production schedules.
- The 2025 FAQ and September 2026 pre-open page differ in broad versus phase-specific market-order treatment. This is resolved architecturally with effective, phase-specific capability rules; account-level current broker confirmation remains required.
- The current NSE generic equity timing page omits the complete CAS lifecycle, while Upstox's market-close event is at 15:30 even though exchange auction settlement/final-price dissemination may occur later. A broker `NORMAL_CLOSE` marker is not sufficient evidence that all auction processing has finished.
- Exact broker intraday square-off deadlines by product/CAS eligibility, margin buffers, order-message counting, enabled segments, client algo status and IP approval cannot be verified without account context.
- Public redistribution and storage rights for broker ticks, full depth, index constituents, weights and calculated derived data were not established. Obtain applicable broker/exchange/index agreements before a multi-user or public release.
- Contract-specific holiday changes, special sessions, emergency halts and later circulars must be checked as part of daily operations. This research is a dated evidence baseline, not a permanently valid calendar.

## Which technology facts support the architecture choice?

### Takeaway

Use a Rust core for market state, features, scanners, risk and OMS, with broker adapters isolated as processes, a TypeScript/React UI and PostgreSQL as the authoritative transaction store. Keep analytics persistence and UI work asynchronous; durable order-intent commit is intentionally part of the execution path and must be measured.

### Cited Findings

- **T01 — Rust.** Rust uses compiler-enforced ownership and has no tracing garbage collector; its official material emphasizes memory/thread safety and performance-sensitive use. This supports predictable allocation discipline but does not establish any latency number for this application. [Rust ownership](https://doc.rust-lang.org/book/ch04-01-what-is-ownership.html); [Rust language](https://rust-lang.org/index.html)
- **T02 — Go.** Go's current collector performs most work concurrently but its guide identifies pause, scheduling, assist and write-barrier costs. Tuning allocation and GC frequency can influence latency; Go is not disqualified from millisecond systems simply because it has GC. [Go GC guide](https://go.dev/doc/gc-guide)
- **T03 — Python.** CPython has supported optional free-threaded builds since 3.13. Extension compatibility and runtime behavior matter, so a blanket claim that every modern Python build has a GIL is inaccurate. This feature does not by itself establish latency predictability for a scanner. [Python free-threading documentation](https://docs.python.org/3/howto/free-threading-python.html)
- **T04 — Bounded queues.** Tokio provides bounded MPSC channels with backpressure and unbounded channels without it. The documented behavior supports explicit queue-capacity policies instead of unlimited memory growth. [Tokio MPSC](https://docs.rs/tokio/latest/tokio/sync/mpsc/)
- **T05 — Database durability.** PostgreSQL synchronous commit waits for WAL to reach permanent storage; asynchronous commit can acknowledge before durable storage and lose recent acknowledged changes on crash. Its documentation expressly advises against asynchronous commit when an external action depends on the transaction being remembered. [PostgreSQL asynchronous commit](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/wal-async-commit.html)
- **T06 — Account coordination.** PostgreSQL offers explicit row and advisory locks, including transaction/session semantics. Locks coordinate database clients; they do not automatically fence a separate broker connection after a network partition. [PostgreSQL explicit locking](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/explicit-locking.html)
- **T07 — Analytical storage.** ClickHouse MergeTree supports sorting/partitioning and TTL rules for deletion, aggregation and movement. TTL is processed in background merges rather than guaranteeing deletion exactly at a timestamp. [ClickHouse MergeTree](https://clickhouse.com/docs/reference/engines/table-engines/mergetree-family/mergetree)
- **T08 — Immutable archive.** S3 Object Lock uses WORM-style retention and legal holds on versioned objects. This is a technical capability, not certification that a chosen Indian-market deployment meets its legal duties. [AWS S3 Object Lock](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonS3/latest/userguide/object-lock.html)
- **T09 — Browser transport.** Browser WebSocket is widely supported and usable from web workers but has no built-in application backpressure. Flooding it can cause memory growth or unresponsive UI. [MDN WebSocket](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebSocket)
- **T10 — React state.** React's `useSyncExternalStore` provides subscriptions to external state with cached immutable snapshots; changing snapshot identity triggers render. This suits a selector-based UI state layer if updates are deliberately coalesced. [React useSyncExternalStore](https://react.dev/reference/react/useSyncExternalStore)

### Inferences

#### Final technology decisions proposed for the blueprint

| Area | Decision | Why / tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| Compute and execution | Rust stable toolchain pinned at implementation kickoff; Tokio for networking, dedicated state-owner workers for CPU work | One type/model system and no cross-language hot-path marshalling; Rust expertise and careful allocation required |
| Broker integration | Independent DhanHQ and Upstox Rust adapter processes, each implementing all required adapter capabilities | Crash isolation and independent reconnect/token/rate budgets; shared contract crate avoids divergent normalization |
| Control API | Rust HTTP server in a separate control process, OpenAPI-described JSON control endpoints | Avoid introducing Go solely for orchestration; backend credentials never exposed to browser |
| Frontend | TypeScript, React, Vite, external-store selectors, virtualized tables, canvas/WebGL heatmap/charts where justified | Incremental updates and clear browser performance budgets; data effects decoupled from rendering |
| Transactional truth | PostgreSQL, synchronous durable commits for OMS intent, fills, risk reservations and account fencing | Transactional constraints and recoverability; DB commit cost is a deliberate execution-path cost |
| Analytics | ClickHouse for ticks/depth/features/scanners/candles, asynchronous buffered writes | Columnar retention/query cost benefits; must tolerate lag and never be the authoritative OMS |
| Archive | Compressed Parquet in encrypted object storage; audit exports with configured immutability | Low-cost retention and repair inputs; no historical trading-replay product |
| Queue baseline | In-process bounded queues plus local framed IPC between adapter/core processes | Avoid serializing/round-tripping every tick through Kafka or Redis; quantify IPC cost |
| Optional Python | Offline formula validation and one-off research tools, outside live service dependencies | Fast exploration without putting Python interpreter/extension behavior on order path |
| Deployment | Linux hosts in an Indian region selected by measured broker RTT, dedicated resources, static approved egress, warm passive standby | Region name is not proof of lowest latency; no unsupported colocation or exchange-fill claim |

**Comparison:** Rust is the recommended complete core. Go-only is a credible simpler concurrency option for a Go-experienced team, but requires allocation/GC tail measurements and would change the core choice; do not offer it as an unresolved parallel implementation. Python-only is convenient for SDKs and formula exploration but would add latency/extension/process-boundary complexity for the specified hot path. Rust+Go is rejected initially because the control workload does not justify an additional language/runtime. Rust+TypeScript is the selected application combination; offline Python is optional, not part of production decision latency.

#### Worker boundaries and ownership

All fifteen requested functions are explicit logical workers; they need not become fifteen network services:

1. **WebSocket ingestion:** per adapter/connection, timestamps frames with a local monotonic clock immediately on receipt; performs lightweight framing only.
2. **Decode/normalize:** per provider, validates schema, scale, lengths and message kind; emits typed updates with source and provenance; corrupt packets fail closed for affected fields.
3. **Instrument state:** partition by stable canonical instrument ID; one writer owns each shard, applying updates and maintaining validity bits, source epochs and feature dependencies.
4. **Candles:** share instrument partition ordering; incremental OHLC/volume accumulators and timer-based finalization; correction events have revisions.
5. **Sectors:** receive changed-constituent contributions, maintain sufficient statistics and freshness coverage instead of rescanning the universe.
6. **Stock ranking:** update affected scores and ordered indexes; pagination/viewport limits only govern presentation, never analysis eligibility.
7. **Scanners:** dependency graph activates only relevant scanner nodes on changed features or completed candles; expensive structures have bounded work budgets and explicit lag.
8. **Regime:** scheduled or change-driven aggregation of breadth/trend/volatility with hysteresis; emits a versioned state vector and uncertainty.
9. **Opportunity scoring:** incrementally refresh candidate score, expiry/invalidation and cost-adjusted feasibility; output can legitimately be empty.
10. **Risk evaluation:** account-owned snapshots/reservations, portfolio exposure and freshness controls; last gate revalidates before durable intent.
11. **Order routing:** one active OMS writer per broker-account; sends only committed intents, owns pending command state, respects independent message budgets.
12. **Order/position updates:** deduplicates and applies broker execution events to that account's order ledger, reconciles against snapshots after ambiguity/restart.
13. **Persistence:** async analytical batches and audit exports; must not consume resources reserved for synchronous OMS commits.
14. **Dashboard publisher:** coalesces latest values, sends sequenced deltas only for subscribed views, supports full snapshot resync.
15. **Health/telemetry:** low-overhead counters/histograms, queues/clock/freshness/decoder state, source availability and broker route health.

Critical compute remains within the instrument/core ownership graph. Sector/regime snapshots are immutable versioned inputs with bounded acceptable age. A new opportunity waits for dependencies only when its policy requires a consistent version; otherwise it is labeled incomplete and cannot execute. Do not block ingress while querying a database, waiting for UI clients or recalculating the entire universe.

#### Queue and overload behavior

- Set capacities from measured burst rate, maximum tolerable queue age and event size. Capacity is not an arbitrary instrument limit.
- Preserve trade/volume-relevant events needed for candles. Do not coalesce away incremental trade volume, OI transitions used by a scanner, executions or risk events. If the upstream feed is quote snapshots, represent observed rather than exhaustive trade information honestly.
- Latest-value coalescing is allowed for UI quote display and redundant snapshots after analytical feature extraction. Record drops/coalescing separately.
- On overload shed optional deep analytics and UI frequency first, then reduce discretionary subscription depth/coverage with visible quality flags. Protective position monitoring and order updates have reserved processing capacity. Unknown account state stops new entries.
- Recovery has jitter, subscription reconciliation, freshness warmup and new source epochs. Never assume there is a provider sequence number or resumable tick history unless broker docs verify it.
- Cross-provider switching needs canonical mapping and a new source epoch; reset/rebase cumulative fields. Do not splice cumulative volume from source B onto source A's baseline.

#### Durable execution and fencing

The candidate-to-submit sequence is: evaluate and reserve risk in the account actor; validate immutable instrument/rule versions; allocate stable intent ID; atomically write intent, reservation, audit linkage and owner epoch in PostgreSQL with synchronous commit; receive durable success; recheck time-sensitive pre-submit guards; route through the sole authorized broker gateway; persist acknowledgment/outcome. Slow durability halts/defer orders; it is not bypassed to hit a benchmark.

Each adapter execution gateway checks an active account epoch and uses a serialized command channel. A database lease alone cannot stop an old process that still holds a valid broker token. Initial deployment therefore uses one active execution gateway, fail-closed on lost ownership proof, and a controlled standby promotion requiring confirmed revocation/fencing of the old gateway plus reconciliation. Infrastructure egress fencing or verified shutdown is required before takeover; no speculative dual-active routing. Do not promise exactly-once order execution across network boundaries.

Order submission timeout after a possible broker send becomes `UNKNOWN_SUBMISSION`, not rejection. Reserve risk and query broker correlation/order history as supported; never blindly resubmit or switch to the other broker. Data-source failover can be automatic after validation; execution-broker selection remains explicit for new order intents, and existing orders/positions remain attached to their origin account.

PostgreSQL WAL crash recovery and ledger reconstruction are operational recovery, not a historical market-replay/backtesting feature. Restore order state against broker order/trade/position/fund snapshots before new entries. Analytics lag cannot become an order truth source.

#### Proposed measurement plan and acceptance targets

These numbers are **engineering targets to validate**, not observed results or broker/exchange guarantees. Publish distinct distributions instead of adding unrelated percentile values:

| Measured path | Proposed p50 | Proposed p95 | Proposed p99 | Maximum measurement / policy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Local frame receive to normalized update | 0.10 ms | 0.30 ms | 0.75 ms | Record observed max per run; alert at 5 ms queue age |
| Local frame receive to candidate/ranking decision | 1 ms | 3 ms | 8 ms | Record max; suppress automated use of candidates older than policy TTL, initial 25 ms for this path |
| Account risk evaluation excluding DB durability | 0.10 ms | 0.40 ms | 1 ms | Record max; never skip a check on overrun |
| Durable intent commit round-trip | 1 ms | 3 ms | 5 ms | Record max; report storage and replication topology; no timeout-to-resubmit conversion |
| Eligible decision to request write, including durable commit | 3 ms | 8 ms | 15 ms | Record max; expire stale intent before send, initially 50 ms policy bound where strategy supports it |
| Server state to visible UI update | 100 ms | 250 ms | 500 ms | Record max and reconnect gap; execution/risk events higher priority |

The candidate path may depend on slower candle-complete or option-chain refresh inputs; sub-10 ms processing does not make stale inputs fresh. Each event carries ingress time, exchange/provider times where present, decision time, enqueue/dequeue times, risk start/end, durable-commit time, send time, acknowledgment and fill times. Local monotonic clocks measure durations; synchronized wall clocks correlate systems. Broker-to-exchange timing is reported only when timestamps are documented and comparable. Avoid subtracting unsynchronized clocks.

Load verification uses generated protocol fixtures, synthetic event bursts and fault injection; it does not create paper trading or a historical trading-replay engine. Test declared hardware, core allocation, kernel/runtime versions, warmup, event mix, instrument count, subscriptions, burstiness, storage commit settings and persistence/UI workloads. Target peak throughput at least twice the measured entitled-feed peak with stable queue age and memory, then identify saturation. Instrument-count values in benchmark fixtures are test sizes, not product limits. Include reconnect storms, malformed packets, slow storage, CPU contention, stalled browser and duplicated/reordered order updates.

Report p50/p95/p99, sample counts, observed worst case, lost/coalesced events, clock uncertainty, queue age, allocation rate, CPU, RSS, GC if Go is evaluated, and recovery time. The acceptance target is valid only within the stated envelope. Never claim a finite worst-case guarantee for a general-purpose OS and external internet broker system.

### Gaps

- No application code or performance benchmark was run. Hardware sizes, achievable throughput and percentile targets need implementation measurement.
- No credentials were provided; order submission/lookup idempotency, broker throttling shape, account entitlements and endpoint behavior must be contract-tested under the authorized account.
- Rust ecosystem crate versions and frontend/library versions should be pinned from supported stable releases at kickoff. This research justifies architecture, not a permanent dependency lockfile.
- Cross-host PostgreSQL synchronous replication changes latency; the deployment must document whether a committed intent survives host/AZ failure and use conservative recovery if the latest authoritative ledger cannot be established. Do not imply local fsync alone guarantees zero loss under total storage failure.
